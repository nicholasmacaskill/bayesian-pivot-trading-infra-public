### 1. AI & Autonomous White-Collar Wage Compression
Historically, home prices in Toronto and Vancouver were driven up by expanding professional salaries (law, tech, finance, consulting). Enterprise AI automation and corporate restructuring are flattening entry-level professional wage growth and cutting middle management. ### 2. Peak Boomer Demographic Liquidation (2035–2045)
Over the next 10 to 20 years, Canada’s largest property-owning cohort (born 1946–1965) reaches ages 80 to 99. Unlike financial speculators, aging estates cannot hold indefinitely:
* Heirs face non-negotiable capital gains tax liabilities on secondary properties with the CRA.
* Heirs frequently lack the personal income qualifications to buy out siblings.
* Specialized senior care costs ($5,000–$8,500/month) demand physical capital liquidation.

This generational turnover creates an ongoing, price-insensitive supply overhang across suburban single-family tracts.

### 3. Carrying-Cost Drag (The Arithmetic of Negative Carry)
When an asset’s nominal appreciation merely matches or trails inflation (2.0%–2.5%), holding that asset results in an **outright financial loss**:
* **Baseline Friction:** Property mill rates, structural maintenance, Capex reserves, and insurance consume **1.5% to 2.0% of property value per year** ($20k–$26k annually on a $1.3M property).
* **Mortgage Service:** Over 25 years, a $1M mortgage consumes hundreds of thousands of dollars in unrecoverable interest.
* **Result:** Sunk capital costs exceed real appreciation. The homeowner functions as an unpaid property manager holding an asset that bleeds real net worth.
